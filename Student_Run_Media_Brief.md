# Student-Run Media Brands & Publications — Research Brief

**Project:** BruinCast | **Date:** June 24, 2026
**Goal:** Benchmark how other schools run student media, and identify what UCLA should build next.

The full dataset lives in `Student_Run_Media_Research.xlsx` (SQL-ready: one table per tab, snake_case columns, integer keys).

---

## The big picture

Across the seven verticals — athletics broadcast, newspapers, radio, video/TV, social, live events, and esports — the strongest schools share one trait: they treat student media as a *connected production system*, not separate clubs. The best programs (Syracuse, Penn State, the Big Ten Network campuses) fold print, radio, TV, and broadcast into one structure where students rotate across mediums and earn academic credit or pay.

UCLA already has the rare asset most schools lack: a single governing body (the Communications Board) overseeing ten titles and 1,000+ students. What's missing is the *broadcast and video* half of the equation.

## What stands out by vertical

**Athletics broadcast** is where the gap is widest and the opportunity is clearest. The Big Ten Network's StudentU program lets students on member campuses produce live games — 2,000+ a year conference-wide — on B1G+, with select games airing on cable, for credit and pay. USC and Penn State already run it. **UCLA joined the Big Ten in 2024 and can plug into the same infrastructure.** Marquette runs a parallel model on ESPN+ with ~30 paid students producing ~60 events a year.

**Video/TV** is dominated by dedicated student studios: Syracuse's CitrusTV (350+ students, five sports shows, the oldest collegiate sports show in the country) and Penn State's PSN-TV. UCLA has no equivalent — video is scattered inside the Daily Bruin and athletics.

**Newspapers** are UCLA's strength. The standout peer trait is editorial independence (The Daily Californian, Columbia Spectator, Harvard Crimson all run independent of their universities) and awards depth (The Daily Texan).

**Radio** best practice is sports play-by-play and podcasts, not just music — Penn State's CommRadio runs 150+ students doing newscasts and live game calls. UCLA Radio is internet-only and music-focused.

**Social, live events, and esports** are emerging. Small student TikTok teams (~5 people) drive outsized engagement; UC Irvine and Boise State show the public-university esports-broadcast model, with Boise State winning NACE Broadcast Team of the Year.

## Recommendations for UCLA (priority order)

1. **Launch a Big Ten StudentU broadcast crew** — highest-leverage move; the infrastructure already exists now that UCLA is in the Big Ten. *(High)*
2. **Start a "BruinTV" student studio** modeled on CitrusTV/PSN-TV to unify video. *(High)*
3. **Add a play-by-play + podcast desk to UCLA Radio.** *(Medium)*
4. **Build a paid student social-content squad** tied to athletics. *(Medium)*
5. **Create an esports broadcast/production unit** (shares crew with #1). *(Medium)*
6. **Add a sports-media vertical under the Communications Board** to coordinate it all — and keep the whole ecosystem trackable in a database. *(Medium)*
