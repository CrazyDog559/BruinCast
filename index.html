Overview

What the benchmarking found, and where UCLA stands.

The pattern that separates strong programs

The best student media operations treat their work as a connected production system, not a set of separate clubs. Syracuse, Penn State and the Big Ten Network campuses fold print, radio, TV and live broadcast into one structure where students rotate across mediums and earn academic credit or pay for it.

UCLA's biggest opportunity: Big Ten Network StudentU

BTN's StudentU program lets students on member campuses produce live games — 2,000+ per year conference-wide — streamed on B1G+, with select games airing on the cable channel, for credit and pay. USC and Penn State already run it. UCLA joined the Big Ten in 2024 and can plug into the same infrastructure (equipment, training, seminars) at low marginal cost.

The clearest structural gap: no student TV station

USC's Trojan Vision reaches 1.8M LA homes via LA36 plus campus cable, runs ~250 students, and draws from six schools — cinematic arts, Annenberg, business, art, music and engineering. Syracuse's CitrusTV runs 350+ students across five sports shows. UCLA has TFT but no comparable student-run channel.

The cheapest win available

UCLA already has an Undergraduate Science Journal — it simply sits outside the Communications Board umbrella. Folding existing orphaned publications into Student Media costs almost nothing and immediately widens the ecosystem.
